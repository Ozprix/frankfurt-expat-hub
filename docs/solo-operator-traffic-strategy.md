# Solo Operator Traffic Strategy

## Positioning

Frankfurt Expat Services should win by being the practical Frankfurt setup layer: free tools, plain-English bureaucracy guides, and a carefully built English-speaking provider directory. As a solo operator, avoid competing with broad relocation agencies on service breadth. Compete on specificity, speed, and useful pages that answer one newcomer problem at a time.

The live search surface still shows the older "Frankfurt Expat Hub" static copy, while the React app is moving to "Frankfurt Expat Services." Finish the migration and keep one brand name across title tags, page copy, schema, sitemap, social profiles, and outreach.

## Traffic Pillars

1. Programmatic directory pages
   - Build one useful page per provider category and city intent.
   - Start with tax advisors, immigration lawyers, relocation agents, insurance brokers, English-speaking doctors, dentists, therapists, driving schools, German teachers, and furnished housing.
   - Each page should include what to ask, price expectations, red flags, documents needed, and a short provider intake form.

2. Tool-led search
   - Keep tools free and index their landing pages.
   - Expand tools around high-intent problems: net salary, Anmeldung document checklist, warm rent calculator, moving cost estimator, Schufa alternatives checklist, German tax ID follow-up tracker, Blue Card document checklist.
   - Every tool page should capture one email with a useful follow-up, not a generic newsletter pitch.

3. Frankfurt-first guides
   - Publish narrow pages that sound like real searches: "tax ID did not arrive after Anmeldung Frankfurt," "temporary housing Anmeldung Frankfurt," "English-speaking Hausarzt Frankfurt public insurance."
   - Keep each article practical, current, and internally linked to one tool, one directory category, and one forum category.

4. Forum as content capture
   - Seed only questions people actually search.
   - Turn good answers into short indexed Q&A articles after they prove useful.
   - Noindex empty forum categories until they have enough original answers.

5. Partner-led backlinks
   - Offer founding directory profiles to English-speaking providers in return for a backlink from their resources page or a LinkedIn announcement.
   - Prioritize small providers who need English-speaking expat leads: accountants, immigration lawyers, insurance brokers, therapists, driving schools, and relocation freelancers.

## 90-Day Plan

### Weeks 1-2: Fix The Base

1. Deploy the React migration so the live site matches the current sitemap and brand.
2. Finish Supabase contact, checklist, reminder, and forum setup.
3. Verify Search Console, Analytics, sitemap submission, robots rules, canonical tags, and index coverage.
4. Create a simple provider intake process: category, city, English level, services, price model, proof links, and preferred lead email.

### Weeks 3-6: Build Pages That Can Rank

Publish two directory pages and two guides per week. Use this order:

1. English-speaking tax advisor Frankfurt
2. Anmeldung Frankfurt checklist
3. Immigration lawyer Frankfurt English-speaking
4. Tax ID after Anmeldung Germany
5. Health insurance broker Frankfurt expats
6. Public vs private health insurance Germany first job
7. Furnished apartment Frankfurt Anmeldung
8. Frankfurt renter packet checklist

### Weeks 7-10: Add Lead Magnets

1. First 30 Days Checklist email delivery.
2. Renter packet checklist.
3. Blue Card appointment document checklist.
4. Health insurance decision checklist.
5. German tax ID follow-up checklist.

Each lead magnet should have one matching article, one tool/interactive checklist, and one directory category CTA.

### Weeks 11-13: Build Supply And Links

1. Contact 10 providers per week with a short founding-listing pitch.
2. Publish one provider interview per week.
3. Ask every listed provider for a backlink or LinkedIn mention.
4. Add trust signals: editorial policy, vetting criteria, correction policy, and last-reviewed dates.

## Weekly Operating Rhythm

1. Monday: update one existing guide with current links and better internal links.
2. Tuesday: publish one new guide.
3. Wednesday: outreach to 10 providers.
4. Thursday: publish or improve one directory page.
5. Friday: review Search Console queries and turn one impression-heavy query into a better section or new page.

This is a sustainable solo cadence: two meaningful content assets, one update pass, and one provider outreach batch per week.

## Page Template

Every commercial or guide page should include:

1. The direct answer in the first 100 words.
2. Frankfurt-specific steps, offices, vocabulary, and timing.
3. A "what to prepare" checklist.
4. Red flags or common mistakes.
5. One relevant tool or downloadable checklist.
6. One provider directory CTA.
7. Last reviewed date and source links to official pages where requirements can change.

## What Not To Do Yet

1. Do not spend time on broad "moving to Germany" articles unless they have a Frankfurt angle.
2. Do not build paid products before organic traffic and provider supply are working.
3. Do not publish thin AI articles. Thin pages will dilute the directory.
4. Do not list providers without transparent vetting criteria.
5. Do not make the homepage carry every SEO query. Let specific landing pages do that work.
